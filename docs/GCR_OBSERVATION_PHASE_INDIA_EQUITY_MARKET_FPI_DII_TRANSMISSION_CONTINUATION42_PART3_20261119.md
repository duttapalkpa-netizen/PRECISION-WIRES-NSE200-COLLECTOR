# GCR Observation Phase — India Equity Market FPI/DII Transmission Evidence (Continuation 42, Part-3)
Date: 2026-11-19
Stage: OBSERVATION ONLY

## 1. Purpose and boundary
This increment records published RBI evidence connecting foreign portfolio flows to Indian equity returns and domestic-investor resilience. It does not estimate a new model, infer causality beyond the cited study designs, set country/factor weights, or alter production trading logic.

## 2. RBI empirical evidence: unexpected FPI flows and equity returns
RBI Bulletin, “State of the Economy” (January 19, 2023), Annex 1, “Impact of FPI Flows in Indian Equity Markets”:
- FPI ownership in NSE-listed equities declined from 23.3% in March 2015 to 19.0% by end-September 2022.
- DII ownership rose from 10.4% to 14.8% over the same period, as DII inflows outpaced FPI inflows.
- RBI decomposed weekly normalized FPI flows into expected and unexpected components using a five-variable VAR, with data from January 2008 to December 2022. It then estimated bivariate VARs of unexpected FPI flows and Nifty 50, Nifty Midcap and Nifty Smallcap returns, separately before and after March 2015.
- Before March 2015, a unit unexpected increase/decrease in FPI flows was associated with a 2.2%–3.0% increase/decrease in market returns over the next 2–3 months.
- Since March 2015, the reported impact declined to 1.25%–1.50%, indicating a lower FPI influence on market returns; the impact became more symmetric across large-, mid- and small-cap indices.
- The source defines normalized FPI flows using the previous 12-week rolling average of total market capitalization; the VAR includes excess NSE500 returns over MSCI EM in USD, India-US relative short-term risk, relative valuation and India-US 3-month T-bill differential.
These are source-study estimates under its definitions and sample. They are not daily effects, not a universal coefficient, and not a guarantee of contemporaneous market direction.

## 3. RBI Financial Stability Report — observed resilience and domestic offset (published 2025-12-31)
The RBI FSR Chapter I reports:
- During calendar 2025 through December 10, DII inflows were ₹7.4 lakh crore versus FPI outflows of ₹1.6 lakh crore.
- The report describes subdued volatility relative to other markets, reduced US-India co-movement/declining beta in recent data, and increased resilience during identified risk-off episodes despite foreign selling.
- RBI attributes support to strong DII demand and growing DII ownership; this is a domestic offset channel, not evidence that external shocks no longer matter.
- FPI remained net sellers of Indian equities cumulatively for a fifth year; RBI links recent relative underperformance to risk-adjusted dollar returns, tepid earnings, higher valuations, tariff outcomes and INR depreciation.
- Tariff event study: broad indices remained relatively resilient, but sector indices responded heterogeneously in April and August 2025. Within banks, trade-exposed corporate lending exposure was associated with larger negative returns for some individual banks; aggregate Bank Nifty volatility was limited. This demonstrates sector/issuer exposure heterogeneity, not a uniform “India market” response.

## 4. GCR observations supported by these sources
1. FPI flows matter, but their market-return relationship is regime-dependent and the reported influence declined after March 2015 in the RBI study.
2. Domestic institutional flows/ownership can cushion foreign selling; the FPI and DII channels must be modelled separately.
3. Aggregate index resilience can coexist with large cross-sector and issuer-level dispersion.
4. Relative attraction must include both external push factors and India-specific pull/resilience: earnings, valuation, domestic institutional demand, risk premia, currency, and trade exposure.
5. Country-level macro shock alone is insufficient to infer stock selection. The transmission chain must be tested at index, sector and security level.

## 5. What is not established
- The RBI 2023 Annex 1 VAR estimates are not independently replicated here; the source study's “unit” normalized-flow shock must not be misread as a one-percentage-point FPI flow or a one-day return.
- FSR narrative and its cited event study do not provide a full raw event-window panel in this report; no independent sector/security abnormal returns have been calculated.
- No causal estimate for any individual country → India → sector path is established.
- No optimal country weights, factor weights, score thresholds, predictive edge or short-horizon (1–3 day) signal are established.
- The evidence does not validate the 154-country panel or the prior 63-country Q2 cohort for aggregate publication.
- Brazil Dataflow mismatch, India Q1-2026 liability revision bridge, Chile vintage bridge, and South Africa exact national series mapping remain as recorded in the master ledger.
